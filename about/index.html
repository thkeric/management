01 · The short version

A designer who asks why it costs that much.

I studied Interactive Media at the University of York, then Design and Digital Media at the University of Edinburgh, where I graduated with merit and was programme representative for my year. Between the two degrees I served in the Republic of Korea Army as a public affairs specialist, making training and promotional videos from first idea to final edit.

Six years of studying and living in the UK gave me a habit I still have. I can't buy something without asking where it was made, how it got here and why it costs what it does. I read local communities and press in English rather than in translation, and the gap between how people talk about a brand in Korea and in the UK is often the most useful signal.

I'm drawn to the small things people notice without knowing why: an icon nobody can decode, a number that doesn't add up, a request that feels awkward to send. Finding the real problem behind them, then owning the fix, is the work I want to do.

02 · How I make calls

Five steps. The last one is the hardest.

The same loop runs through every case in the collection.

  1. Ask firstSurvey, interview or watch before deciding what the problem is.
  2. Name the real problemRewrite the brief as a fact someone can act on.
  3. DecideChoose on evidence, schedule and quality, even when not everyone agrees.
  4. Build and measureShip something real and pick the number that shows whether it worked.
  5. Reverse when wrongWhen the data contradicts me, I change course, including on work I'm proud of.
Call I got wrong

As team lead on an MSc web project, I first tried to keep both teammates happy. The first submission scored poorly and the disagreement grew. Including every view was putting off the decision, so I set the scope myself on schedule and quality, and explained it to the teammate who disagreed. The project received one of the highest marks in the department and was selected for the graduation exhibition.
