Colourway 01 · Lilac

Getting student feedback acted on

Programme representative, University of Edinburgh. Oct 2024 – Nov 2025 · represented 100+ students, unpaid

50students surveyed to size each issue
3proposals adopted by faculty
1 termfor the changes to take effect
The brief
Pass students' feedback to faculty and the programme office.
Real problem
For my first term I passed on what I heard, and nothing changed. "This module is bad" is a feeling, not a request. Faculty couldn't tell one person's complaint from a common problem, so they had nothing to act on.
What I did
Rewrote each complaint as a fact: "the schedule makes no sense" became "deadlines cluster in the same week". Surveyed 50 students to put a number on each issue, then submitted written proposals with alternatives instead of passing things on verbally.
Result
Three proposals were adopted, and curriculum and programme changes were made within one term.
What I learned
Without authority, you move people by handing them something they can decide on.

Colourway 02 · Field olive

Turning one-off videos into a production system

Public affairs specialist, Republic of Korea Army. Nov 2022 – May 2024 · military service

20+training and promotional videos, plan to distribution
10 daysper video, down from two weeks
3departments coordinated
The brief
Plan, shoot, edit and distribute training and promotional videos with the admin, training and public affairs departments.
Real problem
Every video started from scratch, so time and quality varied from one to the next. Scenes that worked on the storyboard often couldn't be shot on the day.
What I did
Introduced a storyboard template and a production schedule to fix the repeated steps, and checked at the planning stage whether each scene could be shot and edited. After each release I collected soldiers' reactions and adjusted the next video's opening length and order of information.
Result
Average production time fell from two weeks to ten days per video.
What I learned
Reducing the variation between videos mattered more than the speed.

Colourway 03 · Shuttle lime

Growing a club by cutting my favourite content

Co-captain, University of York badminton club. Sep 2020 – Jul 2022

20 → 50members over two academic years
0 → 200Instagram followers during my tenure
£200annual budget, every item reconciled
The brief
Co-run the club, including its social channels and its budget.
Real problem
The posts I spent most effort on, competition results, got the weakest response. Recruitment notices and training schedules, which led straight to action, did best.
What I did
Logged the response to each content type and cut back the one I liked most. Ran the new Instagram for incoming students and the established 2,000-follower Facebook page for existing members, each in its own tone. Merged scattered sign-up and enquiry routes into one link, and reconciled every expense under university rules.
Result
Membership grew from 20 to 50 over my two years as co-captain.
What I learned
Newcomers and regulars need different things from the same brand.

Colourway 04 · Deep teal

BudgetBills

Bill-splitting finance app. Oct – Dec 2024 · research, Figma prototype and usability testing

Real problem
Splitting a bill is hard because someone has to ask, not because of the maths. 47.5% of 40 survey respondents named talking about money as the challenge; only 20% reported regular disputes.
What I did
Cut the scope to four core areas, built an animated Figma prototype and ran moderated tests with three participants.
What I owned
The bill flow scored 3/3 in testing and still didn't solve the problem, because the user still had to raise it. My own review flagged it, and I redesigned the concept so the app writes the request and sends the reminders.
The business caseConcept, not yet tested
Growth
Every split request reaches friends who may not use the app yet.
Main metric
Share of split requests settled within seven days.
Warning metric
Declined requests and reminder opt-outs, so reminders don't become pressure.

Colourway 05 · Indigo

Thryve

Gamified health app, MSc thesis. 2025 · research, brand and a working React Native build

Real problem
The market leaders favour people who are already motivated: Strava is built on competition, Fitbit on owning the device. In my survey, 22 of 30 respondents didn't track their health habitually, and 9 of those used no health tool at all.
What I did
Wrote brand principles first, then learned React Native and built a working app with real accounts. When feedback called the first badges militaristic, against those principles, I rebuilt the system around a robot character I modelled in Blender.
What I owned
My own review of the final build found the core loop missing from the home screen. I had also made the avatar central, although custom avatars ranked last (13%) in my survey. Next time I'd lead with daily quests.
The business caseNext iteration, not yet built
Goal
Keep the less motivated users other health apps lose.
Main metric
Seven-day retention: new users still completing quests a week after sign-up.
Warning metric
Notification opt-outs and uninstalls after a missed day.

The drop log

What broke. What held.

Four drops from the collection, logged the way a product team logs a failed test.

Drop 01BudgetBills

The test passed. The problem didn't.

What broke
The bill flow scored 3/3 in usability testing, but the user still had to ask for the money, which was the problem the research found.
What held
I now check every flow against the research problem, not only against task success.
Drop 02Thryve

I built around my idea, not my data.

What broke
I made the avatar central, although custom avatars ranked last (13%) in my own survey, and the core loop never reached the home screen.
What held
The next version leads with daily quests on the home screen and lets the avatar support them.
Drop 03MSc team project

Agreeing with everyone delayed the decision.

What broke
As team lead I tried to include every view. The first submission scored poorly and the disagreement grew.
What held
I set the scope on schedule and quality and explained it to the teammate who disagreed. The project received one of the highest marks in the department.
Drop 04Badminton club

My favourite content was the weakest.

What broke

What held
I cut them back and shifted to recruitment and training posts that led straight to action.

A fandom campaign.

#MyFirstTappy was a spec concept for the English-language webtoon platform Tappytoon's tenth anniversary. It asks fans to share the first title that hooked them. I gave each channel one job, planned a three-week rollout and set a rule: after week one, cut what underperforms and double down on what fans share.
